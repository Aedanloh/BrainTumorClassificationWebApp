Glioma

What is it?

A type of tumor that starts in the glial cells of the brain or spine. Glial cells support, protect, and nourish neurons.

Anatomical Location

Commonly develops in the cerebral hemispheres (cerebrum), but can also form in the brainstem, cerebellum, and optic nerve.

MRI Characteristics

Usually appears as a poorly defined, infiltrative mass. High-grade gliomas (glioblastomas) typically show thick, irregular contrast enhancement with a central necrotic core.

Meningioma

What is it?

A tumor that arises from the meninges—the three layers of membranes that surround and protect the brain and spinal cord.

Anatomical Location

Typically located on the surface of the brain (extra-axial), often along the dural sinuses, sagittal crest, or skull base.

MRI Characteristics

Well-circumscribed, round dural-based mass that enhances intensely and uniformly following contrast. Often displays a "dural tail" sign.

Pituitary Tumor

What is it?

An abnormal growth in the pituitary gland—a pea-sized endocrine gland located at the base of the brain that regulates essential hormones.

Anatomical Location

Located inside the sella turcica, directly below the optic chiasm at the base of the skull.

MRI Characteristics

Microadenomas (<10mm) appear as hypointense lesions on T1 post-contrast compared to normal pituitary tissue. Macroadenomas (>10mm) may expand the sella and display a classic "snowman" configuration.

No Tumor (Normal)

What is it?

Healthy brain MRI scan. No abnormal growths, lesions, masses, or tissue deviations detected within the cerebrum, cerebellum, or brainstem.

Anatomical Checkpoints

Symmetrical brain structures, clear ventricular spaces, intact midline alignment, no mass effect, normal tissue signal intensity across T1 and T2 sequences.

Clinical Recommendation

Continue standard observation. In clinical workflows, classification scans are verified by radiologists alongside patient clinical symptom histories.
